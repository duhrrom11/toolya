Understand the area of a round pan

For round pans with a similar filling height, scale ingredient quantities using the ratio of their base areas: (new diameter ÷ original diameter)².

Example: 10-inch recipe into an 8-inch pan

The factor is (8 ÷ 10)² = 0.64. A recipe calling for 250 g flour would scale to about 160 g. Keep units consistent and check whether pan depth is comparable.

Check baking conditions separately

Changing a pan size may also change baking time and cake height. Monitor doneness rather than assuming time scales with ingredient quantities.

Compare springform, loaf and tray sizes

Measure the inside of each pan. For a straight-sided rectangular pan, multiply its inside length by its width. To keep the batter at a similar depth, divide the target pan's base area by the original pan's base area.

A 25 × 10 cm loaf pan has a base area of 250 cm². A 20 cm round pan has an area of about 314 cm². The factor is about 1.26, so 200 g of flour becomes about 251 g. For sloping sides or a pan with a centre tube, check capacity at a comparable fill level.

More springform conversion examples

20 cm to 28 cm

For round pans, calculate (28 ÷ 20)² = 1.96. This turns 200 g of flour into 392 g and 100 ml of milk into 196 ml.

26 cm to 28 cm

The scaling factor is (28 ÷ 26)² ≈ 1.1598. You need about 16% more batter for the same height: 200 g of flour becomes approximately 232 g, and 150 ml of milk becomes approximately 174 ml. In the other direction, (26 ÷ 28)² ≈ 0.8622, so 200 g becomes about 172 g.

26 cm to 24 cm

Use (24 ÷ 26)² ≈ 0.852. This changes 250 g of flour to about 213 g and 150 ml of milk to about 128 ml. Round after multiplication.

26 cm springform to 30 × 40 cm baking tray

The springform's base area is about 531 cm²; the tray has 1,200 cm². The factor is approximately 2.26, so 200 g of flour becomes about 452 g. This comparison is only valid for similar batter depth.

Which pan measurements do you need?

Always measure inside the pan. Straight-sided springform pans need their inside diameter; rectangular pans need inside length and width. For Bundt pans and tapered loaf pans, compare capacity at a similar fill height because the centre tube and sloping walls affect volume.

Round springform

Base area = π × (inside diameter ÷ 2)².

Rectangular or loaf pan

Base area = inside length × inside width. Check the batter depth.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert a springform recipe for a loaf pan?

Yes. Compare the round pan's inside diameter with the loaf pan's inside length and width. The area-based factor assumes a similar batter depth.

How accurate is conversion for a Bundt pan?

A diameter-based circle approximation does not account for the centre tube or sloping sides. Check the pan's capacity and filling level.

Can I use just the conversion factor?

Yes. Enter both pan sizes and multiply each recipe quantity by the displayed factor.
