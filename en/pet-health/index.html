Keep a pet health and vaccination diary

Organise dog and cat profiles, vaccinations, veterinary visits, weight records and pet-care expenses in one place.

Language availability: This guide is in English. The linked application has not yet been fully translated; some controls and account messages may appear in German. Some features require a free Toolya account.

Getting started

  1. Create a profile for each pet

    Sign in and record the pet's name. You can also add the species, microchip details, veterinary practice and an image of an existing pet passport.

  2. Record vaccinations and appointments

    Add a health diary entry, choose the pet and record the vaccination date and name. If you know the next booster date, enter it separately.

  3. Track visits, weight and costs

    Record a veterinary consultation, optional receipt photograph and paid amount. Use the history and pet filters to review entries over time.

Use reminders as an aid

A recorded booster date helps you plan, but it does not establish which vaccine your pet needs. Follow the advice of your veterinarian.

Pet-care supplies

Use the pet pharmacy area to record supplies you have on hand. Check labels, expiration dates and professional advice before giving medication.

Optional expense transfer

You can transfer eligible paid expenses using Toolya Connect. Set up the household budget in the same Toolya account, save the diary entry and confirm the transfer. Receipt photos and health details stay with the original entry. Check the payment method in the household budget, where it initially appears as cash.

Export an appointment to your calendar

Open Google Calendar from the diary entry or download its ICS file. Check the appointment details before saving or importing. The export uses the date of the entry. If you are planning a future booster, verify the intended date separately.

Frequently asked questions

Can I record several animals?

Yes. Each pet can have a separate profile and history.

Does Toolya issue an official vaccination certificate?

No. It helps you organise records; official veterinary documentation remains separate.
