CHAPTER 1

Who exactly is Romi?

I’m from Luxembourg, born in 1970, and live on the beautiful Moselle on the German side of the border. My day job is a rather boring office job. I’m not a trained programmer. I’m friendly – at least after my first coffee – sometimes chaotic, fond of sarcasm and occasionally clumsy. When a project grabs me, I disappear into it. Whether I’m a chaotic perfectionist or a perfectionist chaos merchant remains scientifically unresolved.

Something you might not expect: I won national titles in artistic gymnastics and later badminton. Granted, Luxembourg is a tiny dot on some world maps. But a title is a title! These days life revolves around work, home and my partner. In summer I head for the water; in winter, the sofa. Two weeks of winter around Christmas are plenty.

CHAPTER 2

When the internet still squeaked

My first encounters with computers were the Commodore 64 and Olivetti. My first hours online were a reverse moon landing: one giant leap for Romi, one small step for mankind. I remember squealing modems and the joy of finally seeing a postage-stamp-sized picture load.

My first website? A photo, chaotic text, an even more chaotic background and extremely annoying background music. We chatted on ICQ and IRC. The internet was a playground for inventors, tinkerers and visionaries. I miss that freedom. I love how the web makes everyday life easier today, but I’m frustrated by how often that turns into another subscription business.

CHAPTER 3

All I wanted was to find some gifts

All year I discover things I’d like. Then someone asks what I want for Christmas or my birthday and my mind goes completely blank. That’s why I love Amazon wish lists. But to surprise someone, I first had to ask for their link. So much for the surprise.

I started building a solution for family and friends. Once the basics worked, I had a perfectly modest idea: why not a directory of public wish lists from around the world? Lists must be intentionally shared publicly. That idea grew into Helferlein and eventually Toolya.

CHAPTER 4

From Helferlein to Toolya

In November 2026 Toolya began taking shape. Alongside wish lists came other helpers for things like household budgets, cake pans and moving boxes. Eventually the foundations of twelve tools were there. Visually, though, it looked like a slightly confused nerd’s collection. So I decided to make it prettier and easier to use.

A new name alone doesn’t make a platform. The tools needed a shared design, reliable technology and a home of their own. Wish list importing, sign-in and small mobile screens were real challenges. My education is learning by doing – especially learning by failing. I can fix things until they break. It’s practically a talent.

CHAPTER 5

Two fluffy colleagues

The redesign gave me the idea of a mascot. A turquoise-and-white creature with goggles became Toolya. I wanted users to have a choice, so the purple Toolina joined in. Two identities, two personalities. They got emotions, outfits and even a tickling feature.

What happens next? Perhaps a little romance. Perhaps not. We’ll see. The two have been part of the platform since the mascot release on 14 November 2026. Neither has requested a holiday yet.

CHAPTER 6

Idealist or hopeless romantic?

I’m frustrated that seemingly every little app costs money. To me, the internet should serve us. That’s why Toolya exists. I cover its running costs myself. I occasionally share my Ko-fi link and Amazon products; voluntary support makes my day, but is never a condition of using the tools.

I want my tools to stay free. No paid unlocks. I also wish that powerful digital opportunities such as AI were available regardless of someone’s bank balance. Of course, technology costs money. That’s precisely why this choice matters to me. Signing in helps with synchronisation and account features; our privacy notice explains how data is processed.

CHAPTER 7

The Yogasutra development method

I work on a laptop or tablet on the sofa. After a few hours I sometimes find myself in a position somewhere between yoga and the Kama Sutra. My partner gives me time for myself and occasionally untangles me. Meanwhile my legs and feet keep moving. Apparently I find that relaxing.

AI helps me develop. Sometimes neither of us seems capable of understanding what the other wants. Still, it’s fun. My motto: It’s not a bug, it’s a feature! Of course the tools should work reliably, and I take real bugs seriously. Toolya isn’t perfect. But it has heart and soul. I want you to feel that.

CHAPTER 8

Give me back the eighties!

Neon fashion, music, people actually talking rather than staring at phones. And hair! I had hair back then. My favourite film is Crossroads (1986), and I always sympathised with Al Bundy. I wish I’d seen Queen, the Guns N’ Roses of those days and Live Aid. My taste ranges from hard rock and heavy metal to alternative, punk, ska and occasionally classical.

And if you’re wondering what I eat: extreme Hawaiian pizza. Pineapple, banana and Mettwurst sausage. Mettwurst goes with everything. Objections are unwelcome and pointless. You may still use Toolya if you disagree.

CHAPTER 9

To be continued

My next big goal is retirement. My partner and I hope to move somewhere sunny in the south. No luxury, just a simple life. As long as it’s warm. A sofa, coffee and internet would be useful, too.

Toolya keeps evolving. I want to share new ideas, genuine mishaps and small victories here. Perhaps you’ll recognise yourself in some of these stories. Thanks for coming along for the ride. – Romi
